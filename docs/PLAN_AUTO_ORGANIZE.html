Organize — auto-layout on the graph context menu

Editor layer only: one pure module (src/modeler/vpl/autoLayout.ts) plus menu wiring. No compiler, no schema, no worker message — node positions are never compiled, so every emitted surface must come out byte-identical. Written plan: PLAN_AUTO_ORGANIZE.md.

1 · The menu — in the editor's own style

Two entry points, both built with the existing .contextSubmenuTrigger hover pattern already used by Align ›, Distribute › and Morph into ›. The trigger row itself has no click action in any of the three, and Organize keeps that rule — the leaves do the work.

Why no "Parameters left / inline" style. GenesisCA draws flow left → right (both flow handles are right-pointing triangles) and every value node feeds an input on its consumer's left edge. Blueprint Assist's ParameterStyle=LeftSide is therefore the only correct behaviour here, not a choice — offering the alternative would put a backwards wire on every parameter.

Third style · Tidy — the non-destructive one

Tidy does not re-layer. It clusters the columns you already made from their current x, keeps your row order, then aligns each column, straightens the flow chain onto its predecessor's handle row, separates the overlaps and snaps to the 20 px grid. Reach for it on a graph you have already arranged.

3 · How the columns are assigned

Two passes. Flow first, left → right from the roots; then value nodes are pulled left of the node that consumes them. A value edge never pushes a flow node right — that is what keeps the exec spine at the column it earned.

Ordering inside a column

  • Seed = the node's previous y (your own intent, kept where it is free), ties by id.
  • 4 barycentre sweeps (down, up, down, up) with flow edges weighted ×4 — a flow chain should come out straight, a value wire may bend.
  • Crossings counted after each sweep; the best ordering seen wins, the seed breaks ties.
  • Fixed sweep count, stable sorts, no randomness ⇒ deterministic and idempotent.

Straightening — the quality step

  • A node's desired y puts its mainFlowIn handle on the same absolute row as its predecessor's taken flow-out handle.
  • Handle offsets come from CaNode's own constants over getEffectivePorts: header centre 15 + (renamed ? 21 : 0); body port i at 30 + (renamed ? 21 : 0) + i·22.
  • Each column is then resolved by the priority method — move toward the desired y, then one monotone push-down / push-up separation that restores the gap without reordering.

4 · Containers — groups are a constraint, comments follow their content

Groups carry no parentId — membership is purely geometric (a node whose centre sits inside the group rect) and is computed only at drag-start. A programmatic move of a group therefore leaves its contents behind. Decision G1 removes that whole class of bug.

Reroutes are preserved, never created. They are transparent to the layout (their edges resolve through to the real source, exactly as the compiler's collapseReroutes does), then placed back on the straight line between their resolved endpoints at the parameter t they had before. Blueprint Assist's bCreateKnotNodes — auto-inserting knots on long or backward wires — is deferred: it mutates the graph, not just positions.

5 · The interaction, end to end

No confirmation dialog. The project's idiom for a reversible bulk edit is undo (Dissolve Reroutes, Create Macro, Morph into, Align, Distribute all commit immediately), and a prompt on every Organize would make a safe action feel unsafe. The genuinely cautious path is the Tidy style, which preserves the arrangement you already made.
