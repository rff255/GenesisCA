The finding that drives everything: the goal — cells that glue together, divide, and form 3D multicellular shape — is not one model but a synthesis of four separable tiers: (I) center-based agent mechanics (the substrate), (II) an explicit dynamic bond graph (the "glue" — persistent labelled edges, not whoever-is-near), (III) division as a geometry-driven graph-rewrite (a physically-realizable split axis; bonds inherited by which daughter ends up on each partner's side), and (IV) a reaction-diffusion field + curvature feedback (the autonomy that makes branching emerge). Each tier maps to a real, verified primary paper.
Substrate verdict: there are two faithful realizations of "the connection topology." (A) center-based + an explicit bond graph (soft spheres + labelled dynamic edges) — lighter, fits GenesisCA's per-cell compiler, GPU-native, 3D-clean, delivers glued chains/clusters/branching — build this first. (B) a vertex model (cells share faces; the face network is the topology made geometric) — true deformable shape + gap-free tiling + real curvature, but the per-cell-step compiler partially breaks because the integrated degree of freedom is a shared vertex. The one honest catch: a soft sphere has no shape, so the faithful Hertwig long-axis division lives in (B); (A) gets a well-grounded tension-axis proxy. Build (A); reach for (B) when shape-driven sheet mechanics becomes non-negotiable.

01 The vision is a synthesis of four tiers

None of the four is speculative — each is a separable layer the literature already treats independently, and each is a real differentiator. Built bottom-up, every tier is independently demonstrable.

02 Two ways to make "the connection topology" concrete

"Cells glued into a dynamic topology" has two faithful realizations, differing on one axis: is a connection an explicit edge between cell centres, or the shared geometry of two cells' boundaries? That axis decides fit, cost, and what shape you can build.

03 Division is a geometry-driven rewrite — not a free choice

The centrepiece, and the place to be most precise. A dividing cell does not freely partition which bonds go to which daughter (no physical plane can keep 10 neighbours on one side and 2 on the other). The split is a physically-realizable axis; bond inheritance follows from the geometry.

04 Live demo — glue, grow, divide along the tension axis

A 2D bond-graph model running in your browser: soft cells repel on overlap, glue to neighbours (bonds drawn as lines), grow, and divide along their tension axis — daughters inherit bonds by geometry. 05 Mechano-chemical feedback — the autonomy layer

Tiers I–III give a glued, dividing aggregate that grows along mechanical axes. Tier IV is what makes morphogenesis autonomous — branching/tubulation emerge from coupling, not a script. All three papers converge here: the rule must sense the shape it is building and steer.

07 Verdict & roadmap

Build (A) center-based + explicit bond graph first. It fits the compile paradigm, is GPU-native and 3D-clean, reuses the center-based force machinery + lookupTable adhesion, and delivers the headline shapes: aggregates → chains → clusters → branching. Treat (B) vertex as a deliberate later engine behind a shared engine-agnostic IR — reached sooner if faithful geometric long-axis division and true folding shape become non-negotiable (they are exactly what (A) cannot do).

08 Silent-corruption hazards (decide up front)

These fail quietly — each is a real bug class the design must close before any build, surfaced by adversarial critique.

The division reattachment triple-hazard

Division mutates partners' bond lists, creates the daughter-daughter bond, AND seeds overlapping daughters at once — hitting maxBonds overflow, the stale-partner invariant, and post-division Δt overshoot simultaneously. Reject the whole division on overflow; clamp Δt to the combined worst case.

Dangling bond / free-list ABI

A dead agent's slot is recycled; a surviving bond holding a raw partnerId silently springs to a stranger. Needs a partnerEpoch slot-generation tag, "break all bonds on death", and a per-step (not just on-load) stale sweep.

Short-range stability invariant

An attractive Hookean bond with no floor pulls bonded centres together (l→0, the direction blows up). The soft-sphere repulsion must dominate attraction at short range — an explicit required invariant, not an assumption.

Hysteresis & the division axis

Equal bond form/break thresholds flicker every step (form < break is mandatory). And weight the division-axis tensor by bond tension, not uniform packing — the packing principal axis is usually the cell's short axis (divides backwards).

09 Literature (the delta — fact-checked)

The center-based substrate bibliography lives in INVESTIGATION_CENTER_BASED.md. Below are only the works grounding this delta. ✓ confirmed · ~ paper real, some metadata unverified.

✓ Okuda, Miura, Inoue, Adachi & Eiraku (2018) Combining Turing and 3D vertex models … Sci. Rep. 8:2386. — The synthesis blueprint: vertex mesh + discrete Gierer-Meinhardt field; division ⟂ longest axis, faces partitioned by side; (γ,χ) selects undulation/tubulation/branching.
✓ Vikran & Hirashima (2025) Curvature feedback for repetitive tissue morphogenesis. Sem. Cell Dev. Biol. 173:103633. — Morphogenetic motifs as L-system rewrites; curvature as the master sense→act→re-sense cue; the mandatory actuator time-lag/hysteresis.
✓ Nelson (2009) Geometric control of tissue morphogenesis. BBA Mol. Cell Res. 1793(5):903–910. — Geometry sculpts the morphogen + stress fields that instruct cells; chemoattraction vs autocrine chemorepulsion branching.
✓ Hertwig (1884) the long-axis rule — division ⟂ longest interphase axis.
✓ Minc, Burgess & Chang (2011) Influence of Cell Geometry on Division-Plane Positioning. Cell 144(3):414–426. — Axis = principal eigenvector of cell geometry.
✓ Bosveld et al. (2016) Tricellular junctions … orient mitosis. Nature 530:495–498. — Neighbour-packing geometry is the spindle-orienting sensor.
✓ Wyatt et al. (2015) … divisions oriented along the long cell axis. PNAS 112(18):5726. — Long-axis divisions dissipate stress → homeostatic packing.
✓ Campinho et al. (2013) Tension-oriented cell divisions … zebrafish epiboly. Nat. Cell Biol. 15:1405. — Divisions orient along, and relax, tension.
~ Scarpa/Mao et al. (2017) E-cadherin & LGN align divisions with tension … PNAS. — Tension can override shape (the blend knob). Author list ~ unverified.
✓ Nestor-Bergmann et al. (2019) Decoupling shape vs stress in mitosis. Cell Rep. 26(8):2088. — Shape axis ≈ stress axis; orientation by shape, rate by stress.
✓ Besson & Dumais (2011) Universal rule for symmetric division of plant cells. PNAS 108(15):6294. — Probabilistic shortest-wall rule (the optional stochasticity).
✓ Ghaffarizadeh et al. (2018) PhysiCell. PLOS Comput. Biol. 14(2):e1005991. — The base center-based engine; later spring-adhesion = the explicit-bond precedent.
✓ CompuCell3D FocalPointPlasticity (Reference Manual). — The canonical explicit-link data model: per-link L, λ, MaxDistance, MaxNumberOfJunctions, create/delete through mitosis.
✓ Fletcher, Osterfield, Baker & Shvartsman (2014) Vertex models of epithelial morphogenesis. Biophys. J. 106(11):2291. — The vertex standard: division, T1, T2.
✓ Merks, Guravage, Inzé & Beemster (2011) VirtualLeaf. Plant Physiol. 155(2):656. — Geometry-driven division that partitions the parent's walls between daughters — the wall analogue of bond reattachment.
✓ Prusinkiewicz & Lindenmayer (1990) The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants (map L-systems, ch. 7). — The theoretical origin of division-as-graph-rewrite.
